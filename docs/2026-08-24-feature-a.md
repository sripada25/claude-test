**Me `[verbatim]`:**
> Your flow is right, and I'd lock it: **signup → mandatory profile (manual or resume upload) → profile complete → app access.** It removes the whole "gate generation on completeness" complexity I proposed earlier. Simpler.
>
> But on AI vs script — **script is the expensive choice here, not AI.**
>
> **Why a script fails:** resumes are two-column layouts, tables, embedded graphics, inconsistent section headers. Extracting "skills" via regex across that variety gets maybe 40–60% accuracy. You'd spend weeks and still field complaints.
>
> **The actual cost math:** resume parsing happens **once per user, at signup.** Not per application.
>
> | Operation | Calls at 1,000 users |
> |---|---|
> | Resume parse | ~1,000 total, ever |
> | Cover letter generation | ~5,000 **per month** (free tier alone) |
>
> Parsing is a rounding error — roughly 0.07% of a single day's Gemini quota per user. Your AI cost lives entirely in generation.
>
> **The right pipeline is hybrid:**
> ```
> PDF → text extraction (pdf-parse library — free, deterministic)
>     → Gemini Flash: "return JSON with name, skills[], experience_level, target_role"
>     → pre-fill the form
>     → USER REVIEWS AND EDITS          ← mandatory
>     → save
> ```
>
> That last step matters. Never save parse output blind — the review pass is what actually guarantees no garbage, more than mandatoriness does.
>
> **One trade-off to name:** mandatory profile before app access adds signup friction. Resume upload mitigates it heavily (30 seconds vs 5 minutes), but expect some drop-off.

But the training clause applies to *whatever you send*, so the line isn't "when users grow" — it's **the first time a real person uploads a real resume.** A resume contains name, phone, email, address, employment history. That's exactly the personal data India's DPDP Act governs. Going paid costs ~$10/month and is a launch-day decision, not a scale decision.
>
> **Provider migration (Gemini → Claude) — cheap if done now.** You're already 80% safe because of L028: **you store plain text, not provider-specific formats.** Text is portable to anything. The lock-in risk lives in your *code*, not your data.
>
> 1. **One internal interface.** `AIProvider` with `extractProfile(pdf)`, `generateCoverLetter(profile, jd)`, `structureCallNote(answers)`. Your services call the interface, never the SDK.
> 2. **Thin adapters per provider.** `GeminiAdapter`, `ClaudeAdapter`. Swapping = writing one new file and changing one env var.
> 3. **Prompts as versioned templates in the DB**, not hardcoded strings.
> 4. **Stamp every generated document** with `provider` and `model` columns.
>
> **L028 — Your image-PDF scenario killed my own recommendation.** I proposed `pdf-parse` → Gemini. **Drop the `pdf-parse` step entirely.**
>
> The Gemini API supports PDF input including long documents, and processes PDFs with native vision — so it understands both text and image contents inside documents. Your exact scenario — a screenshot pasted into a PDF — works with zero extra tooling. No Tesseract, no Google Vision, no OCR pipeline.
>
> **And the billing detail is in your favour:** you are not charged for tokens originating from extracted native text in PDFs, and each document page counts as 258 tokens when processed as an image. A two-page scanned resume ≈ 516 tokens ≈ **$0.00015**.
>
> **Known limitations worth designing around:** models may hallucinate when interpreting handwritten text, and aren't precise at locating text or objects spatially. Your mandatory review step (L049) already covers this.
>
> **On your free/paid download split:** good decision, and it improves the storage economics. Free users view and copy text in-app; only paying users generate stored PDFs. R2 usage now accrues **only from paying customers** — cost tracks revenue directly.
>
> **L029 — The honest privacy map.** You can't have zero third-party sharing while using any AI API. The resume has to go somewhere to be processed. What you *can* do is minimize and disclose.
>
> | Party | Sees | Minimization |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Gemini** | Resumes, profiles, JDs — **biggest exposure** | Paid tier (no training). Send only the needed fields. |
> | **Railway** | Everything (infrastructure processor) | Unavoidable; standard DPA applies |
> | **Cloudflare R2** | Stored documents | Server-side encryption default; only paid users' docs land here |
> | **Brevo** | Recipient addresses + email bodies | Send-only via API. **Never sync contact lists.** |
> | **Razorpay / Stripe** | Payment data | **Use hosted checkout** — card data never touches your server |
>
> **Things that cost nothing and matter:** never log prompt contents or generated documents · delete uploaded resume files after extraction · account deletion must actually cascade (DPDP requirement) · purpose limitation.
>
> **The conflict to name:** you asked for maximum privacy in L029 and free tier in L059. Those pull against each other. Free tier is fine while there are no real users — but you can't hold both once real resumes arrive.
