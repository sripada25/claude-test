## S-04 · Architecture → `L017`

**You:**
> "Architecture: Modular monolith, single deployable (approved)."

Prompted by your earlier concern:
> "i know monolithic approach is easy at start but once users increase i or other again need to rework from scratch on this which could be a waste of time and complete rework on architecture is required. but i am not sure how much it increases in cost."

**Me `[excerpt]`:**
> This is the most common and most expensive misconception in early-stage architecture, and it's mostly false. **What forces a rewrite isn't monolith-vs-microservices.** It's three other things:
>
> 1. Business logic tangled into UI components and route handlers
> 2. No stable API boundary (so every client change ripples through the backend)
> 3. No migration discipline (so the schema becomes unchangeable)
>
> You can have all three problems in a microservices architecture and none of them in a monolith. Shopify, GitHub, and Stack Overflow are monoliths serving enormous traffic. What they have is *internal module boundaries* — a **modular monolith**.
>
> The correct futureproofing move is: **one deployable, clean modules inside it, a real HTTP/JSON API boundary.** When one module genuinely becomes a bottleneck years later, you extract *that one module*. That's a two-week job, not a rewrite.

## S-05 · API boundary → `L018`

**You:**
> "Api Boundary: yes i will go with your recommendation. but for clarity - i understood you are saying api's are mandatory way that helps communication with frontend (web and mobile) to the backend."

**Me `[verbatim]`:**
> Yes. All business logic lives behind JSON endpoints. The React web app calls them over HTTP. When the mobile app arrives, it calls **the same endpoints** — no backend changes, and you can share TypeScript types between both clients. The frontend becomes "a client," not "the app."
>
> Practically in Next.js: logic goes in `/app/api/*` route handlers → service layer → repository → Postgres. **Never** put business logic in a server component or page — that's the trap that makes mobile painful later.

## S-11 · Sizing math → `L028`, `L031`–`L037`

**You:**
> "read and write calculation - you need to do that math. i assume a resume can be 100KB to 200KB."

**Me `[excerpt]`:**
> **Storage per user:**
> - 30 applications × 5 KB = 150 KB
> - generations: 17 KB (free tier) to 105 KB (heavy Pro)
> - call logs: ~8 KB
> - **Blended: ~200 KB/user/month**
>
> | Users | DB after 12 months (incl. indexes) |
> |---|---|
> | 100 | ~340 MB |
> | 500 | ~1.7 GB |
> | 1,000 | ~3.4 GB |
>
> **Operations at 1,000 users:**
> - Writes: ~110/user/month → 3,700/day → **~2.5 writes/minute average**
> - Reads: ~375/user/month → 12,500/day → **~9 reads/minute average**
>
> Postgres on 1 GB RAM handles *thousands* of operations per second. **You are using roughly 0.05% of capacity.** Compute is not your constraint and won't be for years.
>
> **Your actual bottleneck is Gemini Flash: 15 requests/minute.** Not the daily 1,500 — the per-minute burst. Sixteen users generating simultaneously means someone waits. Combined with ~5s per generation, this makes a **job queue with backoff a Phase 2 requirement**, not an optimisation.

On storage, using your 150 KB figure:

> **Better: hybrid.** Store the generated **text** in Postgres as canonical (3.5 KB — 43× smaller, searchable, versionable), render the PDF client-side on demand, and push to R2 **only when the user explicitly saves a version they want preserved**. Most generations get regenerated or discarded.

## S-15 · Builder correction: Railpack → `L021a`, `L043`

**You:**
> "But can you reverfiy this.. railway updated from nixpack to railpack. confirm and tell me what to do next."

**Me `[verbatim]`:**
> **What changed:** Railpack launched in beta on March 4, 2026, replacing Nixpacks with a Go-based solution using BuildKit. Nixpacks is now in maintenance mode — critical fixes only — and Railpack produces Node.js images 38% smaller. Railway still supports Nixpacks for existing projects, but the default builder for new projects is now Railpack.
>
> **What doesn't change:** your original answer stands. Push a repo with `package.json`, Railway auto-detects and builds, nothing to install. Only the builder's name and internals changed.
>
> **What you should actually care about — a portability conflict:** Nixpacks support is concentrated in self-hosted PaaS tools like Coolify and Dokploy plus a few managed platforms; Railpack is Railway-exclusive for now, and if platform portability matters, a Dockerfile is the safest bet.
>
> That collides directly with **L025** (free lift-and-shift is a standing requirement). Railpack builds don't move off Railway. It's the same category of lock-in that got Supabase rejected — just at the build layer instead of the data layer.
>
> **The fix is cheap:** add a Dockerfile to your project root — Railway auto-detects it and prioritizes it over the default builder, no settings changes needed. One file, and your build runs identically on Railway, Hetzner, DO, or anywhere else.
